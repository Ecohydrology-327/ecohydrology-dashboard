Eco-Hydrologic Trend Interpretation & Regional Context:

Declining annual precipitation (-1.960 mm/year) paired with significantly rising vapor pressure deficit (+0.0205 kPa/year, p=0.013) confirms systemic climatic aridification in Central Oregon rangelands. This creates a compounding "atmospheric drought": moisture supply drops while warmer air increases evaporative pull. In this context, western juniper removal serves as a targeted hydrological adaptation strategy by reducing deep biological water extraction and buffering late-season streamflow.

Pluvial / Wet Years

Elevated winter snowpack and spring rains (e.g., 2010, 2017, 2025). Treated Mays WS produces massive runoff gains (up to +33.7 ac-ft surplus) by shunting surplus water directly past removed canopy into shallow bedrock flow paths.

Typical central Oregon semi-arid conditions (~300–400 mm). Untreated Jensen yields baseline flow (6–11 ac-ft), while Mays yields more than double (18–28 ac-ft), confirming reliable post-treatment release without relying on pluvial events.

Prevalent in central Oregon (e.g., 2007, 2008, 2013, 2015). Encroaching juniper in Jensen sustains transpirational withdrawal, quickly drying streams down to 2–6 ac-ft. Mays maintains baseflow resilience (10–18 ac-ft).
